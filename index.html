Research

Anaptyxis and Copy Harmony in Telugu Verb Stems: A Rule-based Account of Moraic Licensing

Manuscript, 2026

Medial vowels in Telugu verb stems alternate: kudur-, kudir-, kudɐr-. Earlier accounts store these alternating vowels and use stress to contextualize the harmony. I argue that they are not stored at all. The roots are consonant clusters, syllabification leaves stray the consonants that can't be licensed as moraic codas, anaptyxis gives each one a monomoraic vowel, and copy harmony gives those vowels, and only those, the quality of a following short i or e. The account predicts that variants of a word differ in syllable count but not mora count. Each rule is classified as input or output strictly local, and the full grammar is implemented and tested on 70 forms.

Computational Morphophonology Benchmark for Low Resource Languages

With prof. Jeffrey Heinz, Institute for Advanced Computational Science, Stony Brook | May 2025 - Present

Testing low-resource morphophonological transducers that learn phonological feature maps, standardizing data across the 66 low-resource languages and documented phonetic inventories, allophony and phonological processes from literature in 21 of them: Ancient Greek, Catalan, Farsi, Finnish, German, Hungarian, Icelandic, Indonesian, Japanese, Kikuria, Lardil, Lithuanian, Makonde, Modern Greek, Mohawk, Saami, Somali, Swahili, Telugu, Turkish and Vedic Sanskrit. The language profiles include phonological feature system, processes, morphophonological rules and thereof finite-state implementations in Pynini under way.

Verse and Metrical Translation

Rigveda | Book 10 Hymn CXXIX

A Metrical Approximation of the 'Nāsadīyasūktam' in English

The vertical bar marks the caesura

Not the unreal | not then the real had been yet,
not the night sky | not the vault there beyond which;
What kept revolving? | Where? In whose surveillance?
Was unventured there | the river deep, celestial?

Neither death was | nor immortality then;
Not night’s, not day’s | then had yet been horizon;
Airless, the One | which could breathe by Its impulse,
Other than that One, | none could have existed.

Before in darkness | the dark had been engulfed,
when unforeseen | like a sea had this all been;
By emptiness held, | the One who emergent,
Emerged alone | from the heat then tremendous.

A wish had turned | within the womb this infant,
A seed of mind | of the kind first who had been.
They found the bond | from real into the unreal,
The wise with wisdom | within beared who witness.

Apart then snapped | traversing the string theirs,
So what was then under? | what was thereover?
Were there the makers? | or were miracles there?
Was autonomy | from before, choice thereafter?

Who really knoweth? | Who indeed can today say…
“Whence is Universe | whence is Its creation”?
If Gods were sequent | to own their creation,
Who thus knoweth | whence this all could have arisen?

Regardless whence | this all could have arisen…
Perchance created… | or whether perchance not…
The One—the witness | in heaven the highest…
Perchance he knoweth | What if perchance he doeth not?
