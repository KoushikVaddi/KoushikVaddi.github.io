Research

Anaptyxis and copy harmony in Telugu verb stems: a rule-based account of moraic licensing

Manuscript, 2026

Medial vowels in Telugu verb stems alternate: kuduru-, kudiri-, kudere-. Earlier accounts store these vowels and use stress to explain which of them harmonize. I argue that they are not stored at all. The roots are consonant clusters, syllabification leaves stray the consonants that can't be licensed as moraic codas, anaptyxis gives each one a monomoraic vowel, and copy harmony gives those vowels, and only those, the quality of a following short i or e. The account predicts that variants of a word differ in syllable count but not mora count. Each rule is classified as input or output strictly local, and the full grammar is implemented and tested on 70 forms.

Computational phonology benchmark

With Prof. Jeffrey Heinz, Institute for Advanced Computational Science, Stony Brook

A benchmark of morphophonological data for testing models that learn phonological maps. I standardized transcription and formatting across the 66 languages in the dataset and wrote literature reviews for 21 of them: Ancient Greek, Catalan, Farsi, Finnish, German, Hungarian, Icelandic, Indonesian, Japanese, Kikuria, Lardil, Lithuanian, Makonde, Modern Greek, Mohawk, Saami, Somali, Swahili, Telugu, Turkish and Vedic Sanskrit. Each review covers the inventory, allophony and allomorphy, feature system and processes, compared against textbook data, with rules formulated for each and finite-state implementations under way.

Recitation

I recite Greek and Vedic verse in reconstructed pronunciation, in the meter, and with modal melody informed by what is known of ancient practice. The long-term aim is to record the whole Iliad and the whole Ṛgveda. The reconstructions are my own working choices, and each recording notes the main ones.
